Why training drains, and what would energize it

R1 landed the mechanics — start to celebration, hands free, four taps. The session still doesn’t feel energizing. That is a design problem, and it will not be solved by putting XP in more places.

n = 2 users 1 real session no analytics most of this is inference

Part one

Four questions

1

Why is it hard to keep training?

  • The cost is immediate and vivid; the payoff is delayed and invisible. Adaptation shows up in 6–10 weeks. Discomfort shows up in 30 seconds. Nothing pays you back on the same day unless the session itself is designed to. inferred
  • How you feel during it predicts adherence better than why you started. In-task affect beats intentions and self-efficacy over 6–12 months, and turns sharply unpleasant above the ventilatory threshold. A program that is accidentally maximal every day is writing the memory that predicts quitting. research
  • You remember the peak and the ending, not the average. So the cool-down and the last screen aren’t admin — they are the memory being written, and that memory is what decides tomorrow. research
  • Progress you can’t perceive doesn’t motivate. People quit while progressing, because nothing told them they were. inferred
  • Goals expire; identity doesn’t. Which is the one thing a defanged streak is genuinely good at. research
2

Why does it feel draining rather than just hard?

Hard and draining are different, and confusing them is how a program gets gentler when it should get clearer.

  • Effort without meaning. The same set costs less when you know what it’s for. Unexplained work is toil.
  • Effort without a visible endpoint. Not knowing how much is left raises perceived exertion at an identical workload. research
  • Cognitive load. Deciding, counting, remembering weights, driving the app — all drawn from the same tank as the effort. The best thing a trainer gives you is that you stop thinking.
  • Dead time that isn’t recovery. Boring gaps don’t restore momentum, they dissipate it, and the next set starts cold.
  • A program that never asks how you are. Identical work on five hours’ sleep costs more — and more again because nothing acknowledged it.
3

Why do some trainers bring out your best?

The design brief, in the most useful form available: everyone has felt both kinds. See the scorecard below for how FitDuo does against it.

4

So how does it become something you look forward to?

  • Design for the memory, not the session — one engineered peak, and an ending that lands.
  • Keep the average pleasant. Hard parts short, framed, finite. Hard days chosen, never accidental.
  • Make progress visible weekly, which is the timescale on which it actually exists.
  • Autonomy inside the frame. A prescribed program is strong on competence and zero on autonomy — exactly the profile of a program that works and gets abandoned anyway. research
  • Zero decisions before the start, about one inside it.
  • Visibly respond when someone tells you something, or they stop telling you.

Part three · the bill

The app traded witness for flow

R1’s contract is right: the app calls the reps, so you stop thinking. The cost is written honestly in the code — assumed: true on every set logged without a human confirming it. Follow that flag downstream and it has a consequence nobody chose.

every uncorrected set is assumed → bestE1rm skips assumed sets → no new records → PR bonus, First PR and Record Breaker are unreachable

Skipping assumed sets is correct — an unwitnessed number must not set the bar real sets have to clear. But the only thing that now produces a witnessed set is an adjustment, which is usually someone recording a miss. The reward system went quiet as the price of the hands-free flow. Which also means the fix isn’t “add rewards” — restoring witness and restoring reward are the same lever.

Part five

Seven principles

Meant to be quotable in a future PR, when something plausible should be declined.

1
Notice before you reward.

A point awarded by a system that didn’t watch is worth less than a sentence from one that did. Prevents: solving an attention problem with an economy.

2
The prize for good work is better work.

Progression, a harder variant, a heavier bell — not a badge. Prevents: reward inflation, which then has to keep inflating.

3
Never spend attention the set itself needs.

Anything motivational competes with the rep being performed. It belongs in rest, changeover, the gate, or the ending.

4
Say the thing the app already knows — if it’s true.

Three of the five most tempting “we already have this” facts turn out not to be known at all. Prevents: a number whose only witness is the code that computed it.

5
A light day is a feature, not a failure.

Hard days are chosen. Prevents: the accidentally-maximal program that teaches people to dread it.

6
Design the ending, because that’s what gets remembered.

Prevents: finishing a good session on a receipt.

7
One screen, two bodies.

A move that can only address one person — counting along, “one more”, “you look flat” — goes in copy both can hear, or nowhere. Prevents: designing a coach for a solo app and shipping it to a couple.

Part seven

What we will not build

Each of these is plausible, cheap and wrong. Decline by citation.

  • XP, points or confetti during a set. Attaching payment to something already done willingly weakens the willingness — and these two already train daily.
  • A live rep counter derived from planned tempo. It would lie by the setup time, double-count unilateral work, and can’t be either person’s count on a shared timer.
  • More achievements. Thirteen is already more than two people will notice.
  • Praise after every set. Discounts to noise inside a week. Specific and occasional, or not at all.
  • Streak anxiety. No loss-framed reminders, no crumbling flame. It stays defanged: freezes, recovery credit, short sessions counting in full.
  • Surprise rewards on physical effort. Slot-machine scheduling on a thing that hurts builds dread, not anticipation.
  • A mascot with feelings about your absence.
  • A leaderboard. Two people in a relationship, never a scoreboard.

Part eight

What would prove this wrong

Worth writing down before we start believing it.

  • They keep training happily and ignore every piece of rest-screen content we add. Then the dead time was fine and boredom was never the problem.
  • Breaking the streak collapses adherence. Then the black-hat drive was load-bearing all along.
  • The sessions they rate best are the ones this predicts should feel worst — long, silent, maximal. Then affect-during isn’t the operative variable here.
  • Voice lands and nothing else matters. Then this over-theorised something R2a solves on its own — the cheapest outcome, and a perfectly good one.

docs/JOURNEY.md is canonical. This page is a summary of it and makes no claim that isn’t there — including the signal table, which exists specifically to stop someone shipping a number the code can’t actually support.

The honest summary of the evidence: one session, two people, two observations, one measured time-split, and a code path traced to a consequence nobody intended. Everything else here is reasoning, and should be treated as the weakest part of any argument it appears in.
